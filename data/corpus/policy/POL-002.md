## Purpose and Scope

The Data Classification Policy defines how Commercial Bank labels and protects information according to its sensitivity. The Data Classification Policy applies to all information in any format, including documents, databases, emails, reports, chat messages and data sent to AI tools. Every document in the bank knowledge base carries exactly one of the four classification levels defined below.

## The Four Classification Levels

Commercial Bank uses four data classification levels, from least to most sensitive: public, internal, confidential and restricted. When information could fit more than one level, the higher classification level applies. Information with no label must be treated as internal until the owner classifies it.

## Public

Public information is approved for release outside Commercial Bank and would cause no harm if disclosed. Examples of public information are published product brochures, interest rates on the website, press releases and the Leave and Remote Work Policy. Public information needs no special handling, but only the owner may change it.

## Internal

Internal information is for Commercial Bank staff and approved contractors only. Disclosure of internal information would cause limited harm. Examples are most policies, architecture documents, runbooks, incident postmortems and meeting notes. Internal information may be shared with any employee, stored on bank systems, and must not be posted on public websites or personal cloud storage.

## Confidential

Confidential information is limited to staff who need it for their role. Disclosure of confidential information could cause significant financial, legal or reputational harm. Examples are customer personal data, security incident details, HSM and key management details, vulnerability reports and fraud detection rules. Confidential information must be encrypted at rest and in transit, shared only with named roles such as analysts and administrators, and never pasted into external AI tools.

## Restricted

Restricted information is the most sensitive category and is available only to a named list of individuals approved by the information owner. Disclosure of restricted information could cause severe harm, breach regulation or affect market-sensitive decisions. Examples are board papers on mergers and acquisitions, details of active security investigations, and privileged credentials. Restricted information requires access logging, must not be downloaded to personal devices, and must not be indexed by any search or AI assistant for users outside the approved list.

## Roles and Access by Classification

For the enterprise AI assistant and knowledge base, the classification levels map to roles: a viewer may access public and internal documents; an analyst may also access confidential documents; an administrator may access all four levels, including restricted. Access to restricted documents is additionally logged and reviewed monthly by the security team.

## Ownership and Review

Each information owner assigns the data classification when creating a document and reviews it at least annually. The CISO owns the Data Classification Policy and approves any change to the four classification levels.
