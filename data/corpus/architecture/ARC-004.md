## Overview

The Data Platform Architecture describes how Commercial Bank collects, stores and serves data for reporting, analytics, regulatory returns and machine learning. The data platform is owned by the data engineering team led by Yusuf Karim. The data platform consists of ingestion pipelines, a data lake in object storage, the enterprise data warehouse, and serving layers for dashboards and models.

## Ingestion

The data platform ingests data in two ways. Batch ingestion loads the nightly core banking extract files (accounts, loans, deposits, customers) after the core banking batch completes. Streaming ingestion reads the payment-events Kafka topic continuously and lands raw events in the data lake within five minutes. Every ingestion pipeline validates incoming data against a registered schema contract.

## Data Lake

The data lake stores raw and cleaned data in object storage in open columnar format, partitioned by date. The data lake keeps raw data for seven years to meet regulatory retention requirements. Confidential and restricted datasets are encrypted with separate keys and access is granted per dataset.

## Data Warehouse and dwh-etl

The enterprise data warehouse holds curated, modelled data for finance, risk and management reporting. The nightly data warehouse ETL, called dwh-etl, runs from 02:00 to 05:30 and builds daily aggregates such as balances, liquidity positions and branch performance. dwh-etl regulatory jobs run in a dedicated reserved compute pool so that other workloads cannot delay regulatory reports.

## Regulatory Reporting

The data platform produces the daily regulatory liquidity report, due to the regulator by 08:00, and monthly capital and credit returns. Regulatory reports are tier 1 outputs of the data platform: any delay to dwh-etl that threatens a regulatory deadline is at least a SEV3 incident and the operational risk team must be informed.

## Analytics and Machine Learning

Analysts use the data warehouse through governed dashboards and a SQL workbench. Machine learning workloads, including fraud analytics models, run on a separate compute pool and read from the data lake. New scheduled workloads in the nightly batch window need a capacity review by data engineering.

## Data Governance

Every dataset on the data platform has an owner and a data classification from the Data Classification Policy. Customer personal data is pseudonymised in analytics zones. Access requests are approved by the dataset owner, and access to confidential datasets is reviewed quarterly.

## Monitoring

The data platform alerts on job failure immediately, on late completion against each job's deadline, and on schema validation failures. Data engineering runs an on-call rota for dwh-etl failures outside business hours.
