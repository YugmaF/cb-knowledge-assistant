## Summary

SEV2 incident. On 2025-12-31 the feature flag instapay_new_router was switched to 100 percent of payments-api traffic in one step, without a canary. The new router sent own-bank transfers to NationalSwitch, which rejected them, from 21:10 to 22:25 on New Year's Eve.

## Impact

The feature flag incident caused failed transfers for about 5,400 customers during New Year's Eve, a high-volume evening. No transfers were duplicated.

## Timeline

- 21:04 Developer enables instapay_new_router for 100 percent of traffic.
- 21:10 Alert: own-bank transfer failures above 30 percent.
- 21:35 SEV2 declared; NationalSwitch rejection code "unknown destination" observed.
- 22:15 Flag disabled by incident commander.
- 22:25 Transfers normal; incident resolved.

## Root Cause

The feature flag incident was caused by a bad configuration change made during the year-end change freeze without canary rollout or change advisory board approval. The flag tool allowed any developer to set a payments flag straight to 100 percent, and the new router had a bug in own-bank routing that a 1 percent canary would have exposed.

## Resolution

The instapay_new_router feature flag was disabled and transfers recovered. The routing bug was fixed and the flag re-released with a canary in January 2026.

## Action Items

- Payments feature flags must roll out 1, 10, 50 then 100 percent with health checks (owner: Daniel Okafor).
- Restrict production flag changes for payments to the release manager role.
- Update the Change Management Policy to cover feature flags (POL-004).
