## Summary

SEV2 incident. On 2025-05-26, the May salary day (moved from Sunday 25 May), the payments-ledger service exhausted its database connection pool between 08:41 and 11:05. Transfers and card payments that post to the payments-ledger failed or timed out for about two and a half hours.

## Impact

The payments-ledger connection pool exhaustion affected approximately 4,200 customers, who saw failed transfers or "payment pending" messages in the mobile app and internet banking. About 11,000 payment requests were rejected with HTTP 503 by payments-api. No funds were lost because failed payments were never debited.

## Timeline

- 08:41 Alert: payments-api 5xx rate above 5 percent.
- 08:52 On-call engineer Kasun Perera finds "unable to acquire JDBC connection" errors in payments-ledger logs.
- 09:20 Incident declared SEV2 and the incident manager is engaged.
- 09:45 payments-ledger pods restarted; the pool is exhausted again within 10 minutes.
- 10:30 Pool size raised from 50 to 120 connections per pod by configuration change.
- 11:05 Error rate back to baseline; incident resolved.

## Root Cause

The payments-ledger connection pool exhaustion was caused by salary-day traffic reaching roughly four times the normal peak. Each payments-ledger pod had a fixed pool of 50 database connections, and a slow balance-recalculation query held connections for up to 8 seconds under load. The payments-ledger had never been load tested at salary-day volume.

## Resolution

The payments-ledger incident was resolved by increasing the pool size to 120 connections per pod and scaling payments-ledger from 6 to 10 pods.

## Action Items

- Add an index for the payments-ledger balance-recalculation query (owner: Ashan Dissanayake, due 2025-06-15).
- Run a salary-day load test before every salary day (owner: Tharindu Jayasuriya).
- Evaluate a connection pooler (PgBouncer) in front of the ledger database (owner: platform team, due Q3 2025).
- Alert when payments-ledger connection pool wait time exceeds 500 ms.
