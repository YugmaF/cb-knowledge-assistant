## Summary

SEV1 incident. At 00:00 on 2025-06-14 the client TLS certificate that the cardnet-gateway-adapter uses to authenticate to the CardNet Gateway expired. All card payment authorisations failed until the CardNet Gateway certificate was renewed at 02:47.

## Impact

The CardNet Gateway certificate expiry blocked all debit and credit card transactions, both point-of-sale and e-commerce, for 2 hours 44 minutes. About 18,500 customers had at least one declined card transaction. ATM withdrawals on the bank's own ATM network were not affected by the CardNet Gateway outage.

## Timeline

- 00:03 Alert: card authorisation success rate drops to 0 percent.
- 00:15 On-call engineer sees "certificate expired" TLS handshake errors in cardnet-gateway-adapter logs.
- 00:40 SEV1 declared; Head of Payments Engineering Daniel Okafor paged.
- 01:30 New certificate requested from the internal CA; Security on-call approval required.
- 02:20 Certificate installed in the adapter keystore; pods restarted.
- 02:47 Authorisation success rate back to 99 percent; incident resolved.

## Root Cause

The CardNet Gateway TLS certificate expired because renewal was tracked in a spreadsheet owned by an engineer who had left the bank. There was no certificate expiry monitoring and no automated certificate rotation for the cardnet-gateway-adapter. The 30-day reminder email went to a distribution list that no longer existed.

## Resolution

The CardNet Gateway certificate was replaced manually with a new one-year certificate and the cardnet-gateway-adapter pods were restarted. Declined card transactions were not retried automatically; customers retried at merchants.

- Add certificate expiry monitoring with alerts at 30, 14 and 7 days (owner: Ruwan Fernando).
